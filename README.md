# ASPIRE: Accurate Alternative Splicing Prediction from Limited RNA Sequencing Data and a Minimal Gene Set

Alternative splicing (AS) is a fundamental biological mechanism that dramatically expands protein diversity and regulates crucial cellular processes across all eukaryotic organisms. Its dysregulation is implicated in numerous diseases, including cancer, neurological disorders, and autoimmune conditions. Accurately predicting AS metrics could improve our understanding of cellular regulation and enhance diagnostic capabilities across multiple pathological states. Current prediction methods predominantly rely on bulk RNA-sequencing, which requires large cell populations and substantial sequencing depth. While single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) enables unprecedented insights into cellular heterogeneity, its limited read coverage often makes AS analysis challenging. This study presents ASPIRE (\textit{A}ccurate \textit{S}plicing \textit{P}rediction from L\textit{I}mited \textit{R}NA S\textit{E}quencing), a novel deep learning approach designed to predict AS metrics, such as Percent Spliced In (PSI), using only the \textit{gene expressions} of limited reads typically available in scRNA-seq data. We identified a small, highly predictive subset of genes by integrating a feature selection mechanism, enabling efficient and cost-effective analysis. ASPIRE addresses the limitations of current scRNA-seq data in alternative splicing analysis by providing accurate predictions with minimal reads. This approach has broad applications beyond cancer diagnosis, including developmental biology, drug response prediction, and personalized medicine. By focusing on a small subset of genes, ASPIRE circumvents the need for extensive data collection while maintaining high prediction accuracy. The versatility of this approach could significantly impact our understanding of splicing regulation in both standard and pathological conditions, potentially leading to improved diagnostic tools and therapeutic strategies across multiple diseases.
